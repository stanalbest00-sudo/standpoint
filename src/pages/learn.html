The Constitution

The Constitution of the Republic of Seychelles, adopted by referendum in 1993, is the supreme law of the country. Any law that conflicts with it can be declared void by the Constitutional Court.

It sets out how the state is organised, how leaders are chosen, and — in its Charter of Fundamental Human Rights and Freedoms — the rights every person holds.

{{i:scale}}

Your rights & freedoms

The Charter protects rights including:

  • The right to life and dignity
  • Equal protection of the law
  • Freedom of expression, assembly and association
  • Freedom of conscience and religion
  • The right to a fair hearing
  • The right to education and health care

Elections

Seychellois citizens elect the President and the National Assembly. Elections are organised by the independent Electoral Commission of Seychelles.

To vote you must be a citizen of voting age and be on the register of voters. Check the Electoral Commission for the latest rules, registration dates and your polling station.

How government works

Power is shared between the Executive (the President, Vice-President and Cabinet), the Legislature (the National Assembly) and the Judiciary (the courts).

Common questions

Quick answers

What is the difference between the Government and the State?
The State is the permanent political community — the Republic itself, its institutions and its Constitution. The Government is the group of people currently elected or appointed to run it. Governments change; the State continues.
Why should I vote?
Voting is how citizens choose who makes decisions on schools, health, jobs and the environment. Every vote counts toward who represents your district and who leads the country.
Who can I contact about a problem in my community?
Start with your District Administration for local services. For national issues, contact your Member of the National Assembly. If you believe a public body has treated you unfairly, the Office of the Ombudsman can help.

How well do you know Seychelles?

Score: 0 / 5

1. In which year did Seychelles become independent?

Seychelles became independent on 29 June 1976.

2. Which body makes the laws of Seychelles?

The National Assembly is the legislature — it debates and passes laws.

3. What is the supreme law of the country?

The Constitution is supreme; laws that conflict with it can be declared void.

4. Who organises elections?

The Electoral Commission of Seychelles is the independent body that runs elections.

5. Which of these is NOT one of the three branches of government?

The media plays an important watchdog role, but the three branches are the Executive, Legislature and Judiciary.
