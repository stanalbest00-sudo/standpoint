Separation of powers

The Constitution divides state power between three branches so that no single person or body holds it all.

Executive

The President & Cabinet

  • Leads the government and runs ministries
  • Proposes policies and the national budget
  • Head of State and Commander-in-Chief
Legislature

The National Assembly

  • Debates and passes laws
  • Approves the budget
  • Questions ministers and scrutinises government
Judiciary

The Courts

  • Interprets and applies the law
  • Settles disputes and tries cases
  • Constitutional Court protects your rights
Checks & balances: the Assembly can scrutinise the Executive, the courts can strike down acts that break the Constitution, and independent bodies (such as the Electoral Commission and the Ombudsman) add further oversight.
Step by step

How a law is made

Every Act of the National Assembly follows the same basic path.

  1. A bill is drafted

    Usually prepared by government, a bill sets out the proposed new law or change.

  2. Published & introduced

    The bill is published and introduced in the National Assembly.

  3. Debate & committee

    Members debate the principles, and a committee may examine it in detail and propose amendments.

  4. Vote

    The Assembly votes. If a majority supports it, the bill is passed.

  5. Presidential assent

    The President signs the bill into law (or may return it to the Assembly for reconsideration).

  6. Gazetted

    The new Act is published in the Official Gazette and becomes part of the law of Seychelles.

Explainer

Who represents you?

Different people represent you at different levels. Know who to go to.

National

The President, elected by all voters across the country.

Constituency

Your Member of the National Assembly, elected by your district to speak for you in the Assembly.

District

Your District Administration, the local office for community services and concerns.
